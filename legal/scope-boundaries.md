# Scope Boundaries

Scope creep kills more engagements than bad work does. It is rarely one big change. It is eleven small favours.

Write the boundaries down before you start, in the statement of work, not in an email.

---

## In scope

Be specific. Name systems, sites, and frameworks.

> **In scope for this engagement:**
> - Assessment of [named systems] against [named framework]
> - Interviews with up to [N] named stakeholders
> - Review of existing documented policies and procedures
> - [N] page written report with prioritized findings
> - One presentation of findings to [named audience]
> - Two rounds of revisions to the report

## Out of scope

This section should be longer than one line. The things you did not think to exclude are the ones that get assumed.

> **Out of scope unless separately agreed in writing:**
> - Any system, site, or business unit not named above
> - Penetration testing, vulnerability scanning, phishing simulation, or any active testing
> - Remediation or implementation of any finding
> - Configuration changes of any kind
> - Certification, attestation, audit opinion, or sign off of any kind
> - Legal advice or interpretation of regulations
> - Incident response, unless separately engaged
> - Vendor selection, procurement, or negotiation
> - Training delivery
> - Ongoing monitoring or managed services
> - Work on any system requiring administrative access

## Assumptions

Assumptions are promises about what they will do. Break one and the timeline moves.

> **This engagement assumes:**
> - Read only access to the systems in Appendix A is granted within five business days of signature
> - A named internal owner is available for [N] hours per week
> - Requested documents are provided within five business days
> - Interviews are scheduled within two weeks of kickoff
> - Existing documentation is provided in a readable format

## What happens when an assumption breaks

> If an assumption is not met, the timeline moves day for day. We will tell you in the same week it happens, not at the end. If a delay exceeds [N] weeks, either party may pause the engagement and we will agree a restart date.

**Why say this:** because it will happen, and saying it up front turns a difficult conversation into a reference to a document both sides already signed.

---

## Change control, in one paragraph

> Any work outside the scope above is a change. Changes are agreed in writing before work begins, with an estimate of hours and any effect on the timeline. Small changes may be absorbed at our discretion. That discretion is not a precedent.

The last sentence matters. It lets you be generous once without setting an expectation.

---

## Handling the eleven small favours

You will be asked. The answer is almost never a flat no.

> "Happy to. That is outside what we scoped, so let me send a quick change note with the hours. Should take me ten minutes to write."

Two things happen. Sometimes they say "actually, do not worry about it," which tells you it was never important. Sometimes they say yes and you get paid. Either outcome is better than doing it silently and resenting it.

## The favours worth doing free

Be generous where it is cheap and it builds trust:

- Answering a question in a meeting
- Reviewing a one page document
- A ten minute call to unblock someone
- Introducing them to a good vendor or peer
- Telling them about something you noticed that was not in scope

Be firm where it is expensive:

- Anything requiring you to log in somewhere new
- Anything producing a written deliverable
- Anything with a deadline attached
- Anything touching a system you were not scoped to see

**The line:** if it takes under fifteen minutes and creates no obligation, do it. If it creates a deliverable or an obligation, it is a change.
