# Handover Checklist

Start this two weeks before the end date, not on the last day.

---

## Two weeks out

- [ ] Confirm the end date in writing with the sponsor
- [ ] Ask directly: "what would make this feel finished for you?"
- [ ] Identify who owns the program after you leave. By name
- [ ] Book the handover session with that person
- [ ] Book the final presentation with the sponsor

**If nobody owns it after you leave, say so plainly.** In writing, once, without drama. Then help them decide between hiring, appointing internally, or a small ongoing retainer. Do not let it go unsaid because it is awkward.

## One week out

- [ ] Every deliverable finished and in the shared folder
- [ ] Risk register current, every open item has an internal owner and a review date
- [ ] Roadmap updated to reflect what actually happened
- [ ] Draft the "what to do next" one pager
- [ ] List every account you were given

## Handover session

Ninety minutes with the person taking over. Walk them through, do not send and hope.

- [ ] Where everything lives
- [ ] The risk register, item by item, and why each is ranked where it is
- [ ] The roadmap, and what the next three actions are
- [ ] The recurring meeting, who chairs it, what it covers
- [ ] The board reporting format and where the numbers come from
- [ ] Vendors and contacts worth knowing
- [ ] The things you would do next if you were staying
- [ ] The things you tried that did not work, and why

**That last one is the most useful thing you can give them.** Nobody documents the failed approaches, so the next person repeats them.

## Final presentation

- [ ] What was found
- [ ] What changed
- [ ] What is left, and who owns it
- [ ] What to watch for in the next six months
- [ ] Say what they did well. Specifically. People rarely hear this

## Last day

- [ ] Written request to disable every account you were given, listed
- [ ] Confirm removal from internal groups, chat channels, ticket systems
- [ ] Return any hardware, badges, or tokens
- [ ] Confirm data destruction. See `data-destruction.md`
- [ ] Final invoice
- [ ] Ask for a referral or a reference while the work is fresh

**Sending the disable list unprompted is a small thing that clients remember for years.** It costs you five minutes and it says everything about how you work.

---

## Thirty days after

- [ ] Short check in email. No agenda, no pitch
- [ ] Ask how the first governance meeting without you went
- [ ] Offer to answer one question free, any time

**Why:** this is not a sales tactic, it is finishing the job. It also happens to be where most repeat work comes from.

---

## Your own review

Write this for yourself, not the client.

- What did I get wrong at the start that I could see by week four?
- Which question in discovery would have saved me the most time?
- What did I under-scope?
- Would I take this engagement again at this price?
- What goes into my process because of this one?

Keep these somewhere private. Reading a year of them back is the fastest way to get better at this.
