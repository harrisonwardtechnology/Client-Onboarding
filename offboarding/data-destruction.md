# Data Destruction

Giving back and deleting what you held. Do it properly and confirm it in writing.

---

## What you are probably holding

People forget most of this. Go through it line by line.

- [ ] Documents they sent (email attachments, shared folder downloads)
- [ ] Exports you pulled from their systems
- [ ] Screenshots in your notes and in the report drafts
- [ ] Your working notes, digital and paper
- [ ] Interview recordings or transcripts
- [ ] Spreadsheets you built from their data
- [ ] Anything in your email, including sent items
- [ ] Anything in chat history
- [ ] Anything in a cloud sync folder that also lives on a second device
- [ ] Anything in a backup of your own machine
- [ ] Anything you pasted into a tool. Including AI tools

**The last two are the ones people miss.** Your own backup will hold their data long after you deleted the file. Say so honestly rather than claiming a clean deletion you cannot deliver.

---

## What to do

| Category | Action |
|---|---|
| Their documents | Delete after the retention period agreed in the contract |
| Your working notes | Keep only what you need for professional liability, usually the final report |
| Report and deliverables | Keep. You may need to defend your work years later |
| Exports and raw data | Delete. There is no good reason to keep these |
| Credentials | Delete immediately, and confirm the accounts are disabled |
| Backups containing their data | Note the retention period honestly. Do not claim instant deletion |

---

## Agree retention in the contract, not at the end

Sensible default:

> Consultant will retain the final deliverables and engagement records for [seven] years for professional liability purposes. All client source documents, data exports, and working files will be securely deleted within [30] days of engagement close, except where retention is required by law. Backups containing client data will age out within [90] days under normal backup rotation.

**Why say it this way:** it is true. A claim of immediate complete deletion is usually false because of backups, and a false claim is worse than an honest retention period.

---

## The confirmation letter

Send this on the last day. It takes two minutes and it lands well.

> **Subject: Engagement close, access and data confirmation**
>
> Hi [name],
>
> [Engagement] is complete as of [date]. Two housekeeping items.
>
> **Please disable these accounts I was given:**
> - [system] - [username]
> - [system] - [username]
>
> **On your data:**
> I have deleted all source documents, exports, and working files you provided. I am retaining the final report and engagement records for [period] for professional liability purposes, as set out in our agreement. Backups on my side will age out within [period] under normal rotation.
>
> If you would like written confirmation in a specific format for your own records, tell me what you need and I will send it.
>
> Thanks for the work. It was a good engagement, and [one specific genuine thing they did well].
>
> [Name]

---

## If you are ever asked to certify destruction

Some clients, especially regulated ones, will want a formal certificate. Ask what standard they need it against before you write anything. Sign only what is actually true.

Never certify destruction of something sitting in a backup you cannot reach. Say what is true instead, and give them the date it ages out.
