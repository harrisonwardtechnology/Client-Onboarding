# Qualifying Call Script

Thirty minutes. No slides. No deck. You are not selling yet, you are finding out if there is a real problem.

## Open (2 minutes)

> "Thanks for the time. I want to spend most of this listening. I will ask what pushed you to make this call, what you have already tried, and who has to be happy at the end. If it turns out I am not the right fit I will tell you and point you somewhere better."

Say that last part and mean it. It changes the whole call.

## The four questions that matter

### 1. "What happened?"

Nobody hires security help on a calm Tuesday. Something happened.

Listen for:
- A customer sent a security questionnaire and they could not answer it
- A deal is stuck in procurement
- Cyber insurance renewal came back with questions or a higher price
- Something got in. Maybe a near miss. Maybe not
- A board member or investor asked a question nobody could answer
- An auditor said a word that scared someone
- Someone left and took the only knowledge of the environment with them

**Why it matters:** the stated ask is rarely the real driver. "We need a risk assessment" usually means "we lost a deal." Solve the real one.

### 2. "What have you already tried?"

Listen for:
- A previous consultant, and how that ended
- A tool they bought and never turned on
- A policy set downloaded and never read
- An internal person who tried and burned out

**Why it matters:** if the last three people failed the same way, you will too unless something structural changes. Find out what that is.

### 3. "Who has to be happy at the end of this?"

Push for names, not titles. Then ask, "who can say yes without asking anyone else?"

**Why it matters:** engagements die when there is no single decision maker. Two sponsors with different definitions of success means you fail for one of them by design.

### 4. "What does done look like?"

If they cannot answer, that is the first deliverable and you should say so.

Good answers sound like:
- "We can complete a customer security questionnaire without lying"
- "Insurance renews without a surcharge"
- "The board gets a real update every quarter"
- "We know what we have and who can reach it"

Bad answers sound like:
- "We want to be secure"
- "We need to be compliant"
- "Just tell us what to do"

Bad answers are not disqualifying. They mean scoping is your first job.

## Questions to ask about you

Ask these. They protect you.

- "What is the budget range you are working with?" If they will not say, you are being priced against a number you cannot see
- "What is the timeline, and what is driving it?" A hard external date changes everything
- "Have you done work like this before?" First timers need more hand holding. Price that in
- "Who else are you talking to?" Not to compete. To find out if they want a person or a platform

## Close (3 minutes)

> "Here is what I heard. [Play it back in three sentences.] If that is right, my next step is a short questionnaire so I can scope this properly. Takes about forty minutes to fill in. Want me to send it?"

Playing it back is the whole test. If you cannot summarize their problem in three sentences, you do not understand it yet.

## After the call, write down

- The real driver, in one line
- The name of the person who can say yes
- What done looks like, in their words
- Your gut. Write it down before you rationalize it

Score it in `go-no-go-scorecard.md` within twenty four hours, while it is still honest.
