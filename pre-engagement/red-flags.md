# Red Flags

Patterns that predict a bad engagement. None of these is automatically disqualifying. Two or three together usually is.

Write down which ones you saw before you decide. It is much harder to rationalize a list.

---

## About the buyer

**No single decision maker.** Two sponsors with different goals means you will fail for one of them no matter what you do. Ask directly: "who can say yes without asking anyone else?"

**The sponsor will not attend the kickoff.** If it is not worth an hour of their time at the start, it will not be worth their time when a hard decision is needed.

**They want the report, not the outcome.** Listen for "we just need something to show the customer." You are being hired as paperwork. Sometimes that is a fine job. Price it as one and be clear about what it is.

**They will not share the driving document.** If a customer contract or insurance application is forcing this, and they will not let you read it, you are scoping blind.

**Nobody will own it after you leave.** Not a dealbreaker. It does mean your job is different: build for the people who exist, not the team they wish they had.

## About scope and money

**Budget is a secret.** You will be priced against a number you cannot see, and you will lose or underquote. Ask twice, politely.

**Assessment budget with no remediation budget.** You will produce findings nobody funds. Ask what happens after the report.

**"Just tell us what to do" with no time to give.** Advice needs someone to receive it. Zero internal hours means zero change.

**Deadline set by someone who is not in the room.** Ask who set it and what happens if it slips. Often the answer is "nothing," which changes everything.

**They want a fixed price for an unknown environment.** Either scope a small paid discovery first, or price the uncertainty in and say why.

## About behaviour

**The previous consultant is described as an idiot.** Maybe. Ask what specifically went wrong. If the answer is vague, you are next in line.

**They push back on read only access.** You asked to look, not to change. Resistance here usually means either extreme dysfunction or something they do not want seen.

**They want you to sign their MSA without changes and without a lawyer.** Anyone can have terms. Nobody should skip reading them.

**Pressure to start before paperwork is done.** "We can sort the contract later" means you will be arguing about scope while unpaid.

**Someone asks you to test something you are not authorized to test.** Never proceed on a verbal yes. See `legal/authorization-letter-template.md`.

## About the work itself

**They want you to attest, certify, or sign off.** Advisors advise. If they need an auditor, they need an auditor. Saying so costs you a deal and saves you a lawsuit.

**They want the assessment to reach a specific conclusion.** Walk. This is the one that is actually disqualifying on its own.

**Scope grows during scoping.** If it moved twice before signing, it will move six times after. Fix the boundaries in writing.

## How to say no well

Say it early, say it warmly, and give them something.

> "I do not think I am the right fit for this, and I would rather say that now than three weeks in. Here is why. [One honest sentence.] What you probably want is [X]. Two people who do that well are [names]. Happy to make an intro."

You lose a deal. You keep a reputation. People remember the consultant who told them the truth, and they come back with the right project later.
