# Communication Norms

Agreed in kickoff. Written down. Boring on purpose.

Most engagement friction is not about the work. It is about someone expecting a reply on Saturday, or not knowing a decision was made.

---

## Cadence

| What | When | Who | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly status | Every [day], by [time] | Consultant to internal owner and sponsor | Short written update, five bullets |
| Checkpoint call | Every [two weeks], 30 minutes | Consultant, internal owner, sponsor | Live |
| Working sessions | As needed | Consultant and relevant team | Live |
| Findings review | At [milestone] | Everyone | Live, 90 minutes |
| Final presentation | End of engagement | Sponsor and audience they choose | Live |

**Put all of these in calendars during kickoff.** Not after.

---

## The weekly status format

Five bullets. Same five, every week. Never longer than a screen.

1. **What done looks like** (unchanged, restated every week on purpose)
2. **Done this week**
3. **Next week**
4. **Blocked, and who owns unblocking it**
5. **Decisions needed from you, with a date**

**Why restate the goal every week:** engagements drift. Repeating the goal in writing every week is the cheapest correction mechanism there is.

**Why name the blocker owner:** "waiting on access" is a complaint. "Waiting on access, owner is [name], since Tuesday" is a fact somebody can act on.

---

## Channels

| Use | For |
|---|---|
| Email | Decisions, deliverables, anything you might need to point at later |
| Chat (Teams, Slack) | Quick questions, scheduling, unblocking |
| Calls | Anything with disagreement in it |
| Shared folder | Documents, drafts, the current risk register |

**The rule:** if a decision happens in chat or on a call, it gets written into email the same day. Otherwise it did not happen.

---

## Response times

Agree these out loud so nobody guesses.

| Situation | Expected response |
|---|---|
| Normal question | One business day |
| Blocked, work stopped | Same business day |
| Something is on fire | Phone, any time. See below |
| Weekend and evening | No response expected either direction, except fire |

**Say this part explicitly:**

> "I will not expect replies in the evening or at weekends, and you should not expect them from me. If something is genuinely urgent, call. Do not email and hope."

**Why:** it protects both sides, and it makes the word urgent mean something.

---

## The 2am question

Answer these in kickoff, not during an incident.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| If something happens out of hours, who calls whom? | |
| What number? Is it in phones already? | |
| Is out of hours support in scope, or is it a separate arrangement? | |
| If email and chat are down, what is the fallback? | |

**Why:** if you are engaged when an incident happens, the first thirty minutes are spent finding people. Answering four questions now removes that entirely.

---

## Escalation path

| Level | When | To whom |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Normal blocker, under a week | Internal owner |
| 2 | Blocked more than a week, or a decision nobody will make | Sponsor |
| 3 | Scope, money, or something is going badly wrong | Sponsor, live call, not email |

**Before you escalate, tell the person you are escalating past.** Every time. It costs one sentence and it preserves the relationship.

---

## Bad news

Deliver it early, in person or on a call, and with an option attached.

> "Two things are not going to land on the original date. Here is why, and here are two ways to handle it. My recommendation is the second one."

**Why:** bad news delivered late is a competence problem. Bad news delivered early with options is just project management. The same fact, read completely differently, based only on timing.
